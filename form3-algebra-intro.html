Lesson 1 of 5

The Language of Algebra

Builds on: Arithmetic and the order of operations (the warm-up). In Unit 1 you also used x to stand for an unknown number in a Venn diagram.

Goal: By the end of this lesson I can name the parts of an expression and translate a word phrase into algebra.

1 Key ideas

  • A variable is a letter that stands for a number we do not know yet, or a number that can change.
  • A term is a number, a letter, or numbers and letters multiplied together. Terms are separated by + and − signs. The sign in front of a term belongs to it. 5x² − 3x + 7 has three terms: 5x², −3x and 7.
  • The coefficient is the number in front of a variable. In 5x² − 3x + 7, the coefficient of x² is 5 and the coefficient of x is −3. The term x has coefficient 1, and −y has coefficient −1.
  • A constant is a term with no variable, such as 7.
  • An expression has no = sign (3x + 2). An equation has an = sign (3x + 2 = 14).
  • How to write algebra: 3 × a = 3a (the number goes first), a × b = ab, a × a = a², a ÷ 4 = a/4, and 1x is written as x.
  • Key words: sum, more than, increased by, add → +. difference, less than, decreased by, subtract → −. product, times, twice, double → ×. quotient, divided by, half of → ÷. square of → ².
  • Watch out for "less than". It reverses the order: "5 less than x" means start with x and take away 5, so it is x − 5, not 5 − x.

3 Worked examples

Example 1.1

Naming the parts of an expression

For the expression 4x² − 7x + 9 − y, state the number of terms, the coefficient of each variable and the constant.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Split the expression at each + or − sign, keeping the sign with the term: 4x², −7x, +9, −y. That is 4 terms.
  2. The coefficient of x² is 4. The coefficient of x is −7 (the minus sign belongs to the term).
  3. −y means −1 × y, so the coefficient of y is −1.
  4. 9 has no variable, so it is the constant.

Answer: 4 terms. Coefficients: x² → 4, x → −7, y → −1. Constant: 9.

Example 1.2

Translating phrases

Write each phrase in algebra: (a) seven more than n (b) 5 less than x (c) the product of 3 and y (d) a number n divided by 4 (e) twice the sum of x and 3

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) "more than" means add: n + 7.
  2. (b) "less than" reverses the order. Start with x and take away 5: x − 5.
  3. (c) "product" means multiply: 3 × y = 3y.
  4. (d) n ÷ 4 = n/4.
  5. (e) Find the sum first (x + 3), then double it. Use brackets to show the sum is done first: 2(x + 3).

Answer: (a) n + 7 (b) x − 5 (c) 3y (d) n/4 (e) 2(x + 3)

Think: Why do we need brackets in (e)? What would 2x + 3 mean in words?

Example 1.3

Order matters

Write in algebra: (a) the difference between 10 and p (b) p less than 10 (c) 10 less than p

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) "The difference between 10 and p" is written in the order given: 10 − p.
  2. (b) "p less than 10": start with 10 and take away p: 10 − p.
  3. (c) "10 less than p": start with p and take away 10: p − 10.
  4. Check with a number. If p = 15: (b) gives 10 − 15 = −5 and (c) gives 15 − 10 = 5. The results are different, so order matters.

Answer: (a) 10 − p (b) 10 − p (c) p − 10

Example 1.4

Expressions from a real situation

Keisha is k years old. Her brother is 4 years older than her, and her mother is three times Keisha's age. Write an expression for each age, and for the sum of all three ages.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Brother: 4 years older, so k + 4.
  2. Mother: three times Keisha's age, so 3k.
  3. Sum: k + (k + 4) + 3k.
  4. Collect the k terms: k + k + 3k = 5k. So the sum is 5k + 4. (You will practise this kind of simplifying in Lesson 2.)

Answer: Brother k + 4, mother 3k, sum of ages 5k + 4

Example 1.5

From algebra back to words

Write each expression in words: (a) 3x − 2 (b) 5(n + 1) (c) y²/2

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) x is multiplied by 3, then 2 is taken away: "2 less than three times x".
  2. (b) n and 1 are added first, then the answer is multiplied by 5: "five times the sum of n and 1".
  3. (c) y is squared, then divided by 2: "half of the square of y".

Answer: (a) 2 less than three times x (b) five times the sum of n and 1 (c) half of the square of y

Common mistakes

  • Writing 5 − x for "5 less than x". The correct expression is x − 5.
  • Writing x2 or x × 2 instead of 2x.
  • Mixing up 2x (x + x) and x² (x × x).

Lesson 2 of 5

Simplifying Expressions and Expanding Brackets

Builds on: Lesson 1: terms and coefficients.

Goal: By the end of this lesson I can collect like terms, multiply and divide terms, and expand a bracket.

1 Key ideas

  • Like terms have exactly the same letters raised to the same powers. 3x and −5x are like terms, and 2ab and 7ba are like terms. x and x² are not like terms.
  • To collect like terms, add or subtract their coefficients. The sign in front of each term belongs to it: 5x − 2x + x = 4x.
  • Unlike terms cannot be combined. 3x + 2y stays as 3x + 2y.
  • Multiplying terms: multiply the numbers, then multiply the letters. 3a × 4b = 12ab, 2x × 5x = 10x², and −4a × 3b = −12ab.
  • Dividing terms: divide the numbers, and cancel letters that appear on the top and the bottom. 15xy ÷ 5y = 3x.
  • Expanding a bracket: multiply every term inside the bracket by the term outside. a(b + c) = ab + ac. An area diagram shows why this works.
  • A negative number outside a bracket changes the sign of every term inside: −2(x − 5) = −2x + 10, because −2 × −5 = +10.
  • In Unit 4 (Factorization) you will do the reverse of expanding: 6x + 12 = 6(x + 2).

3 Worked examples

Example 2.1

Collecting like terms

Simplify 5x + 3y − 2x + 7y − 4.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Group the like terms, keeping each sign with its term: (5x − 2x) + (3y + 7y) − 4.
  2. 5x − 2x = 3x and 3y + 7y = 10y.
  3. −4 has no like term, so it stays.

Answer: 3x + 10y − 4

Example 2.2

Powers make terms different

Simplify 4a² + 3a − a² + 5a − 2.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. The a² terms: 4a² − a² = 3a².
  2. The a terms: 3a + 5a = 8a.
  3. a² and a are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined.

Answer: 3a² + 8a − 2

Think: Test your answer with a = 1: the original gives 4 + 3 − 1 + 5 − 2 = 9, and 3 + 8 − 2 = 9. Why is substituting a number a good way to check your work?

Example 2.3

Multiplying and dividing terms

Simplify: (a) 3p × 4q (b) 2x × 5x (c) −4a × 3ab (d) 15xy ÷ 5y

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) Numbers: 3 × 4 = 12. Letters: p × q = pq. So 12pq.
  2. (b) 2 × 5 = 10 and x × x = x². So 10x².
  3. (c) −4 × 3 = −12 and a × ab = a²b. So −12a²b.
  4. (d) 15 ÷ 5 = 3, and the y on the top cancels with the y on the bottom. So 3x.

Answer: (a) 12pq (b) 10x² (c) −12a²b (d) 3x

Example 2.4

Expanding a bracket

Expand: (a) 4(2x + 3) (b) x(x − 6) (c) −3(2y − 5)

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) 4 × 2x = 8x and 4 × 3 = 12. So 8x + 12. The area diagram shows the two parts.
  2. (b) x × x = x² and x × (−6) = −6x. So x² − 6x.
  3. (c) −3 × 2y = −6y and −3 × (−5) = +15. So −6y + 15.

Answer: (a) 8x + 12 (b) x² − 6x (c) −6y + 15

Example 2.5

Expand and simplify

Expand and simplify 3(2x + 1) − 2(x − 4).

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Expand the first bracket: 3(2x + 1) = 6x + 3.
  2. Expand the second bracket. The −2 multiplies both terms: −2 × x = −2x and −2 × (−4) = +8.
  3. Put them together: 6x + 3 − 2x + 8.
  4. Collect like terms: 4x + 11.

Answer: 4x + 11

Think: Many students write −8 instead of +8 here. What is −2 × −4, and why?

Common mistakes

  • Combining unlike terms, e.g. writing 3x + 2x² = 5x³.
  • Multiplying only the first term inside the bracket: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 4 ✗.
  • Losing the sign with a negative outside the bracket: −2(x − 3) = −2x − 6 ✗.

Lesson 3 of 5

The Laws of Indices

Builds on: Lesson 2: multiplying terms such as x × x = x².

Goal: By the end of this lesson I can use the laws of indices, including zero and negative indices.

1 Key ideas

  • In aⁿ, a is the base and n is the index (or power). aⁿ means a multiplied by itself n times: a⁴ = a × a × a × a.
  • Law 1 (multiplying): aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ. Add the indices. For example, x³ × x² = (x·x·x)(x·x) = x⁵.
  • Law 2 (dividing): aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ. Subtract the indices. For example, x⁵ ÷ x² = x³.
  • Law 3 (power of a power): (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ. Multiply the indices. For example, (x²)³ = x² × x² × x² = x⁶.
  • Zero index: a⁰ = 1 for any a ≠ 0. Why? a³ ÷ a³ = a⁰ by Law 2, but anything divided by itself is 1.
  • Negative index: a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ. Why? a² ÷ a⁵ = a⁻³ by Law 2, and cancelling gives 1/a³.
  • When there are coefficients, work out the numbers normally and use the laws for the letters: 3x⁴ × 5x² = 15x⁶. In (2x³)² = 4x⁶, remember to square the 2 as well.
  • The laws only work when the bases are the same. 2³ × 3² cannot be written as a single power.

3 Worked examples

Example 3.1

Why the multiplication law works

Show that x³ × x² = x⁵ by writing out the multiplication.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. x³ = x × x × x and x² = x × x.
  2. x³ × x² = x × x × x × x × x.
  3. That is 5 x's multiplied together, so x⁵. The indices were added: 3 + 2 = 5.

Answer: x³ × x² = x⁵

Think: Use the same idea to explain why x⁶ ÷ x² = x⁴. Hint: cancel x's from the top and bottom.

Example 3.2

Using the three laws

Simplify: (a) a⁵ × a³ (b) y⁷ ÷ y³ (c) (p²)⁴ (d) 5⁰

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) Multiply, so add the indices: 5 + 3 = 8. a⁸.
  2. (b) Divide, so subtract the indices: 7 − 3 = 4. y⁴.
  3. (c) Power of a power, so multiply the indices: 2 × 4 = 8. p⁸.
  4. (d) Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1.

Answer: (a) a⁸ (b) y⁴ (c) p⁸ (d) 1

Example 3.3

Laws with coefficients

Simplify: (a) 4x³ × 3x⁵ (b) 20a⁶ ÷ 5a² (c) (3m²)³ (d) 2a²b × 5ab³

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) Numbers: 4 × 3 = 12. Letters: x³ × x⁵ = x⁸. So 12x⁸.
  2. (b) Numbers: 20 ÷ 5 = 4. Letters: a⁶ ÷ a² = a⁴. So 4a⁴.
  3. (c) Cube everything in the bracket: 3³ = 27 and (m²)³ = m⁶. So 27m⁶.
  4. (d) 2 × 5 = 10, a² × a = a³ and b × b³ = b⁴. So 10a³b⁴.

Answer: (a) 12x⁸ (b) 4a⁴ (c) 27m⁶ (d) 10a³b⁴

Example 3.4

Negative indices

(a) Evaluate 2⁻³. (b) Simplify x² ÷ x⁶, giving your answer with a positive index. (c) Write 10⁻² as a decimal.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) 2⁻³ = 1/2³ = 1/8.
  2. (b) x² ÷ x⁶ = x²⁻⁶ = x⁻⁴ = 1/x⁴.
  3. (c) 10⁻² = 1/10² = 1/100 = 0.01.

Answer: (a) 1/8 (b) 1/x⁴ (c) 0.01

Think: A negative index does NOT make the number negative. Is 2⁻³ positive or negative?

Example 3.5

Evaluating without a calculator

Evaluate (3⁵ × 3²) ÷ 3⁶.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Multiply the top first: 3⁵ × 3² = 3⁷.
  2. Then divide: 3⁷ ÷ 3⁶ = 3¹ = 3.

Answer: 3

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying the bases: 2³ × 2⁴ = 4⁷ ✗. The base stays the same: 2⁷.
  • Forgetting to apply the power to the coefficient: (3x²)² = 3x⁴ ✗. It should be 9x⁴.
  • Thinking a negative index makes the number negative: 2⁻¹ = −2 ✗. It is ½.
  • Thinking a⁰ = 0. It equals 1.

Lesson 4 of 5

Substitution

Builds on: Lessons 1–3: expressions and indices, and BODMAS from the warm-up.

Goal: By the end of this lesson I can find the value of an expression or formula when I am given the values of its letters.

1 Key ideas

  • To substitute, replace each letter with its value, then work it out using BODMAS.
  • Remember the hidden operations: 3x means 3 × x, and ab means a × b.
  • Put negative numbers in brackets. If x = −4, then x² = (−4)² = 16, but −x² = −(4²) = −16.
  • Indices come before multiplication. If x = −4, then 3x² = 3 × 16 = 48. Square first, then multiply.
  • A formula is a rule that connects quantities, such as v = u + at or P = 2(l + w). Substitute the values you know to find the one you do not know. Include units in your answer.

Brackets and indices

Lesson 5 of 5

Solving One-Step and Two-Step Equations

Builds on: Lessons 2 and 4: expanding brackets and substitution (for checking). In Unit 1, Lesson 5, you also solved equations like 42 − x = 35.

Goal: By the end of this lesson I can solve one-step and two-step equations and form an equation from a word problem.

1 Key ideas

  • An equation says that two expressions are equal. Solving it means finding the value of the letter that makes it true.
  • Think of a balance. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other side, so that it stays balanced.
  • Use inverse operations to undo what has been done to x. + undoes −, and × undoes ÷.
  • One-step: x + 7 = 12 → x = 5. 4x = 20 → x = 5. x/3 = 6 → x = 18.
  • Two-step: undo the operations in reverse order. In 3x + 5 = 20, x was multiplied by 3 and then 5 was added. So first subtract 5, then divide by 3. A function machine shows this. You will use the same idea in Unit 3 to change the subject of a formula.
  • Brackets: either expand first, or divide both sides by the number outside the bracket.
  • Letters on both sides: collect the letter terms on one side and the numbers on the other.
  • Always check by substituting your answer into the original equation.

Example 5.1

One-step equations

Solve: (a) x + 9 = 15 (b) y − 4 = 11 (c) 5k = 35 (d) m/4 = 3

  1. (a) Subtract 9 from both sides: x = 6.
  2. (b) Add 4 to both sides: y = 15.
  3. (c) Divide both sides by 5: k = 7.
  4. (d) Multiply both sides by 4: m = 12.

Answer: (a) 6 (b) 15 (c) 7 (d) 12

Example 5.2

A two-step equation

Solve 3x + 5 = 20.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. What happened to x? It was multiplied by 3, then 5 was added.
  2. Undo in reverse order. First subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 15.
  3. Then divide both sides by 3: x = 5.
  4. Check: 3 × 5 + 5 = 20 ✓

Answer: x = 5

Example 5.3

Negatives and fractions

Solve: (a) 7 − 2x = 1 (b) x/5 − 2 = 4

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. (a) Subtract 7 from both sides: −2x = −6. Divide by −2: x = 3. Check: 7 − 6 = 1 ✓
  2. (b) Add 2 to both sides: x/5 = 6. Multiply by 5: x = 30. Check: 30/5 − 2 = 4 ✓

Answer: (a) x = 3 (b) x = 30

Example 5.4

Equations with brackets

Solve 4(x − 3) = 28.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Method 1: divide both sides by 4: x − 3 = 7, so x = 10.
  2. Method 2: expand first: 4x − 12 = 28. Add 12: 4x = 40, so x = 10.
  3. Check: 4(10 − 3) = 4 × 7 = 28 ✓

Answer: x = 10

Think: Both methods work. When is dividing first quicker? Would it work for 4(x − 3) = 30?

Example 5.5

Letters on both sides

Solve 5x + 2 = 3x + 14.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Subtract 3x from both sides so the x terms are on one side: 2x + 2 = 14.
  2. Subtract 2: 2x = 12.
  3. Divide by 2: x = 6.
  4. Check: left side 5 × 6 + 2 = 32 and right side 3 × 6 + 14 = 32 ✓

Answer: x = 6

Example 5.6

Forming and solving an equation

The length of a rectangle is 5 cm more than its width, x cm. The perimeter is 34 cm. Find the width and the length.

Try it in your book first. Then reveal the solution one step at a time and compare it with your working.

  1. Length = x + 5.
  2. Perimeter: x + (x + 5) + x + (x + 5) = 34, so 4x + 10 = 34.
  3. Subtract 10: 4x = 24, so x = 6.
  4. Width 6 cm and length 11 cm. Check: 6 + 11 + 6 + 11 = 34 ✓

Answer: Width 6 cm, length 11 cm

Common mistakes

  • Undoing the operations in the wrong order, e.g. dividing by 3 before subtracting 5 in 3x + 5 = 20.
  • Doing an operation to only one side of the equation.
  • Sign errors with negatives: in 7 − 2x = 1, the x term is −2x, not 2x.
